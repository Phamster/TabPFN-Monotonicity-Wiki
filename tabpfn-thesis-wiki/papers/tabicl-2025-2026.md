# TabICL / TabICLv2

A competing TFM lineage to TabPFN, designed specifically to address the scalability bottleneck — the original goal was supporting datasets up to ~500K rows (far beyond TabPFN v2's 10K-sample ceiling) while keeping in-context learning intact. TabICLv2 (Feb 2026) iterates further on speed and openness.

An independent industry benchmark (Mission Lane, a consumer lender) tested the first TabICL version directly against a production LightGBM credit-risk baseline on proprietary data — one of the few real-world, non-academic stress tests of a TFM in the credit domain. The post explicitly notes TabICLv2's architectural changes hadn't yet been re-evaluated at the time of writing, leaving an open question about whether the latency/performance trade-offs they found still hold.

## Relevance to thesis

This is the most credit-risk-adjacent *empirical* TFM evaluation outside academia, which makes it valuable as a sanity check even though it's not peer-reviewed. It confirms TFMs are already being seriously evaluated by lenders for production use — which raises the regulatory-compliance question this thesis would address with more urgency than a purely academic framing would. Worth re-running or citing carefully, since it's a blog post, not a peer-reviewed source — use it as motivation/context, not as a citable empirical result.
